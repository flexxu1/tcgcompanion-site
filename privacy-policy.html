TCGPartner Privacy Policy

TCGPartner is a Chrome extension that keeps your data on your computer. It has no account and no sync. Everything it records stays on your machine until you delete it or export it yourself. TCGPartner keeps your cards and lists in your browser. To show current prices, it asks TCGPartner's own price service once a day for the prices of the cards you saved, and of a card whose TCGPartner menu you open on TCGplayer. Apart from the card pictures it shows, which come from TCGplayer, that is the only thing it sends, it is on by default from version 0.8.0, and you can turn it off in Options at any time. What it sends, to whom, and what is and is not logged is described in full below.

TCGPartner is an independent extension and is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or sponsored by TCGplayer. Until 1 November 2026 it was named TCGCompanion. The rename itself changed only the name and the support address, not how the extension handles your data.

Effective date: 23 November 2026 · Applies to TCGPartner (formerly TCGCompanion) version 0.2.1 and later (first published 19 August 2026 for 0.1.0; the cart helper described in earlier versions was removed in 0.2.1). Last verified against the extension source on 23 November 2026 (UTC; version 0.8.0).

Network activity: the daily price update

The extension makes two kinds of network request of its own. The first is stated plainly under Card pictures below: showing a card's picture (a saved card's, or a printing offered after reading a photo or for a pasted or imported card name) loads that image from tcgplayer.com. (The Try Google link below is not a request by the extension: it is a search your browser opens only when you click it.) The second is the daily price update, Daily prices (below): on by default from version 0.8.0 unless you turn it off in Options (in version 0.7.0, off until you turned it on). It never contacts TCGplayer: it asks TCGPartner's own price service for the prices of the products you saved (and of a card whose TCGPartner menu you open on TCGplayer). That service is the only server TCGPartner runs; nothing else of yours is sent to the developer, and there is no cloud sync, analytics, advertising, telemetry, or crash reporting. The extension's own code holds exactly one network call (fetch), in background/price-db-transport.js, used only by Daily prices and able to make only the two requests described below; there is no XMLHttpRequest, WebSocket, EventSource or sendBeacon call anywhere in the extension's own code. The one other piece of code that has a file loader is the photo reader's built-in ONNX Runtime (see Reading a photo or screenshot), which uses it only to read its own files from the extension. The extension's content security policy (connect-src: the extension's own files and, from version 0.7.0, the price service's one host, https://tcgpartner-prices.tcgpartner.workers.dev; in version 0.6.0 and earlier, the extension's own files only) makes Chrome refuse any connection from the extension's pages and worker to anywhere else. If that ever changes, this policy will be updated before the change ships - see Changes to this policy below.

Deck import works from what you type. When you paste a card list into Import, the card names, copies, printing numbers and set codes you pasted are saved exactly as typed, into a deck on your machine; nothing is looked up anywhere. Cards added this way store three extra fields: where the record came from (importedFrom), the printing number you typed (importNumber, with the set total after a slash when you typed one, such as “010/086”) and the set code you typed (importSetHint), each only when present. These are identity only - an imported card carries no price and no picture until you open its TCGplayer page yourself, exactly like any other unpriced card. To tell which imported card a product page is, the extension reads that page's own card number (its “Card Number” or “#” detail) and the game its breadcrumb names (such as “Magic: The Gathering Cards”) while you view or save the card; both are used for that comparison, and to point out an imported card that this page is a variant of (“pick the variant”), and neither is stored. Before version 0.4.0 (2026-09-12) deck import sent the typed names to a third-party card database to offer a printing picker; that call, its host permission and its stored fields are gone, and records written by earlier versions are rewritten to the typed shape on first run.

“Didn't find a match? Try Google.” TCGplayer's search needs the name spelled exactly, so an imported card that it misses gets a second link, on the dashboard and on a TCGplayer search page opened to resolve that card. Only if you click it, your browser opens a Google search in a new tab: https://www.google.com/search with the card name, set code and number you typed plus the word “tcgplayer”. Google receives that text the way it receives any search you type yourself, under Google's own privacy policy. The extension sends nothing to Google itself, has no access to google.com and runs nothing there. So that Use this card can appear on the TCGplayer page you open from the results, the click - and a click on that card's Find on TCGplayer link - also remembers which imported card you were choosing (its storage key, the name you typed and the time) in chrome.storage.session, the browser's temporary extension storage. It is cleared when you use it, when you dismiss it, after 30 minutes, or when the browser closes, and it never leaves your browser.

Price check before a price list. When you export a price list and some of its cards have no price or a price not from today, the export dialog offers Check on TCGplayer. Only if you click it, your browser opens the first of those cards' TCGplayer product pages in a new tab. On each of them a small TCGPartner bar shows the market price the page displays (read the same way as on any visit) and a Next card link; each next page loads only when you click it. To show that bar, the click remembers the check in chrome.storage.session: the list name, up to 30 product page addresses with the storage keys of the cards they price, and the start time. It is cleared when you stop the check, after 60 minutes, or when the browser closes, and it never leaves your browser.

Importing a list file. Import also reads a .csv, .tsv, .xlsx or .xls (Excel), .ods (OpenDocument) or .txt list and MTGO (.dek), Cockatrice (.cod) or Yu-Gi-Oh! (.ydk) deck files: a TCGPartner (or, from before the rename, TCGCompanion) price list or price sheet, a TCGplayer app collection or Seller Portal export, a ManaBox, Collectr, Moxfield or Deckbox CSV, a spreadsheet, a deck file, or a plain card list. You can pick the file or drop it on the import panel. The file is read in your browser (an Excel or OpenDocument file is unpacked there too, with no library and no upload; a .ydk file names cards only by passcode, and their names come from a card list built into the extension, from Yugipedia under CC BY-SA 4.0, so nothing is looked up online) and its cards are added to a list as written, with importedFrom set to list-file. When a TCGplayer file names the exact copy's SKU (the seller export's “TCGplayer Id”, the app export's “SKU”), that number is kept as importSkuId so the card's price list can list it again; it is dropped if you change the card's condition, printing or language. A price in the file is not stored as the card's price; it is added to the card's note (“Price on imported list: ...”), as is a printing the extension does not track, and a language that would otherwise relabel a card you already saved (“Language on imported list: ...”). The one exception is a TCGplayer Seller Portal pricing export: its TCG Market Price column is TCGplayer's market price, so it becomes the market price (currentMarketPrice, marketObservedAt and a price-history entry) of each saved card it matches, marked with lastPriceSource import and dated when the file was exported (from its file name, or else the file's own modified time). A file or pasted text with no such date never replaces a price read on TCGplayer; it only prices cards that have none. Importing such a file updates the prices of cards you already saved that it matches. Cost columns (what someone paid) are not read. Nothing is looked up anywhere.

Reading a photo or screenshot. Photos are read on this computer and never uploaded. The import panel's Add photos reads a photo or screenshot of cards (one card, a binder page or several cards on a table) or of a card list (picked, pasted or dropped on the panel). The picture is read on your computer: the text reader (PaddleOCR models run by ONNX Runtime, both built into the extension) loads from the extension's own files only when you give it a picture (picked, pasted or dropped), and neither the picture nor the text read from it is sent anywhere. The text goes into the import box, where you check and correct it before anything is added; words the reader was unsure of are marked. A Yu-Gi-Oh! card's name is checked against the same built-in card-name list the .ydk import uses, and every card's text against a built-in copy of TCGplayer's card catalog for its game (Pokemon, Magic: The Gathering, Yu-Gi-Oh!, Disney Lorcana, One Piece; nothing is looked up online). How the card looks is compared too, on your computer: the extension ships small fingerprints of each printing's picture (about 16 bytes each, no images), made in advance from public card databases' images – Scryfall (Magic), Lorcast (Disney Lorcana), TCGdex (Pokemon, MIT licence), YGOPRODeck (Yu-Gi-Oh!) and OPTCG API (One Piece), never TCGplayer's. A fingerprint of the card in your photo is made on your computer and compared with them; nothing is downloaded for this and the photo never leaves your computer. TCGPartner is not affiliated with or endorsed by those databases or the games' publishers. TCGPartner is unofficial Fan Content permitted under the Fan Content Policy. Not approved/endorsed by Wizards. Portions of the materials used are property of Wizards of the Coast. ©Wizards of the Coast LLC. When the photo does not show which printing a card is, the most likely printings are shown for you to pick from, each with its picture, set, number and name. Those choice pictures are loaded from TCGplayer's image server, as on any TCGplayer page (see Card pictures), so that server sees which printings' pictures were shown; the photo itself never leaves your computer, and neither does the text read from it. The picture, and small pictures of any card it could not read, are shown to you while you check the result and are then forgotten: they are not saved, and only the card lines you add are stored, exactly like typed ones.

Price lists export one list, or all your cards, as a spreadsheet (.csv) or a plain-text file (.txt). The spreadsheet has each card's quantity, name, set, card number, printing, condition, language, price (marked when it is an estimate), TCGplayer product id and SKU id and, only when a card has one, your asking price, a seller's price and an offer from an imported list, your note, whether you collected it (not on a selling list) and, for all your cards or Likes, the lists it is in. The text file has one line per card (quantity, name, set and number, condition and printing, price and your asking price) under one line naming the list, and a last line saying the product uses TCGplayer data but is not endorsed by TCGplayer (it names TCGplayer as the source of the prices only when every price in the file was read on a TCGplayer page). It is a file your browser downloads. Every feature is free; nothing about tiers or licences is stored.

Prices are read from TCGplayer pages you already have open in your own browser session. The extension never fetches TCGplayer pages by itself.

Card pictures. When the dashboard shows a card's picture, your browser loads that image from the address stored with the card - TCGplayer's own image servers - the same request the site itself makes when you browse. The printings offered after reading a photo, or for a pasted or imported card named without its set (a name with several printings is asked: “Which printing is this?”), load their pictures the same way, from the product id in the built-in catalog, as soon as the choices are shown, as on any TCGplayer page. Nothing but the image address is sent. That is the only host ever loaded from; a backup naming any other host renders “No image” instead.

Sealed product pictures. A box, pack or tin is cut out of its picture's backdrop on your computer: by an outline shipped with the extension for that product, or else by a small built-in model (TensorFlow.js, loaded from the extension's own files) run on the picture the dashboard already shows. Nothing is sent for this. So the model runs only once per picture, the outline it finds (a few hundred bytes of shape, no pixels) is kept in the extension's own IndexedDB database (tcgpartner-sealed-outlines) under that picture's address. It is not in backups, and Erase everything in Options deletes it.

Daily prices (on by default from version 0.8.0)

Which versions offer it: Daily prices are on by default from version 0.8.0. In 0.7.0 they were off until turned on, and Options showed a disclosure before anything was sent; 0.6.0 and earlier do not have them: those versions cannot reach the price service and none of what follows happens in them. Updating from 0.6.0 or 0.7.0 to 0.8.0 turns them on, unless you turned them on and then off again in 0.7.0: that choice is kept. Someone whose update turns them on sees one line in the dashboard the first time they open it after the update: Daily prices are now on. Turn off in Options. It stays until they close it. Nothing is sent before that line has been shown.

Daily prices show TCGplayer's price for each saved card in its own condition, printing and language without opening each card's page. Daily prices are on by default. This is what happens:

TCGPartner asks its own price service (on Cloudflare Workers, run by Cloudflare, Inc.) for the prices of your saved cards: once a day, and a few seconds after you save new cards. When you open TCGPartner's menu on a card on TCGplayer, it also asks for that one card, so the menu can show the printings that card has. Each request sends TCGplayer product numbers and nothing else: no account, no cookie, no card names, conditions, quantities, lists, prices or notes of yours, and nothing you typed. The service sees those product numbers together with your IP address, the time and your browser's standard headers, so it can tell which TCGplayer products you saved or looked at through the menu. It does not log or keep the product numbers with your IP address: to stop abuse it keeps only a count of today's lookups under a scrambled form of your IP address, and deletes it the next day. A day's update is about 16 KB for 200 cards and about 115 KB for 2,000 cards. Nothing is sent to TCGplayer. No request is made until you save a card or open the menu on one; after an update from an older version, nothing is sent until the dashboard has shown its one line about the change, and the first update then asks for the cards you already saved.

To turn it off: Options, Daily prices. That stops every one of these requests at once and deletes the stored prices. Prices are then read only from the TCGplayer pages you open.

  • What is sent: two kinds of request, both to the price service and nowhere else - GET /v1/manifest (at most about once a day: the date of the day's prices and the default estimate percentages) and POST /v1/prices, whose body is {"ids":[...]}: the TCGplayer product numbers of your saved cards, at most 250 per request, or the one product number of a card whose TCGPartner menu you opened on TCGplayer. The extension checks the body against exactly that shape before it sends anything. No cookies or credentials, no referrer; a redirected request is refused.
  • When: from version 0.8.0, the first time the extension starts with saved cards (for someone updating from 0.6.0 or 0.7.0, when the dashboard shows the line about the change); once a day after that; a few seconds after you save new cards (only their product numbers); when the dashboard shows a card whose price is not stored yet; when you open TCGPartner's menu on a card on TCGplayer that is not stored yet (that one product number); and when you turn the feature back on. With no card saved and no menu opened, nothing is asked at all.
  • What the service sees, and what is and is not kept: the product numbers in the request, your IP address, the time, and your browser's standard headers (including the extension's own ID, sent by Chrome as the request's origin). The product numbers are used only to answer that request: they are not written to any log, database or analytics, and the service's request logging is switched off. To stop abuse it counts how many product numbers were looked up from each IP address each day; the IP address is kept only as a one-way hash that changes every day, next to that count, and the count is deleted the next day. Cloudflare, which runs the service, handles each connection as any web host does, under its own privacy policy.
  • What is kept on your computer: the prices of your saved products, in the extension's own IndexedDB database (tcgpartner-daily-prices), with when they were looked up. They are public prices, not your data: they are not in backups, and turning daily prices off (or erasing everything in Options) deletes them. Which printings each saved product has (names only, no prices) is also kept in the extension's local storage (chrome.storage.local, meta_product_printings), so a card's chips stay the same with daily prices off; it is not in backups, and erasing everything in Options deletes it. Your settings for the feature are stored with your other settings: whether you turned it off, whether the line about the update is still to be shown or has been closed (version 0.7.0 also stored when you accepted its disclosure; 0.8.0 keeps from it only that you turned the feature off again, as off, and drops the rest), and your own estimate percentages if you set any.
  • What the prices are: TCGplayer market prices, compiled once a day by TCGPartner's price service from public price data, shown as TCGplayer price. This product uses TCGplayer data but is not endorsed or certified by TCGplayer. Where TCGplayer has no market price for a card's own condition, TCGPartner can show an Estimate (the Near Mint price times a percentage you can change), always labelled, never added into a total, and marked as an estimate in exported price lists. A price read on the card's own page replaces an estimate, and replaces a daily price when it is newer.
  • Daily prices are part of the free extension and are not behind any paid tier.

What the developer collects

With Daily prices off, nothing: the developer does not collect, receive, transmit, sell, rent, or share any user data, and the extension never sends your information off your device. With Daily prices on (the default from version 0.8.0), TCGPartner's price service receives the TCGplayer product numbers of your saved cards (and of a card whose TCGPartner menu you open) with your IP address, uses them only to answer each request, and keeps neither, apart from the daily lookup count under a one-way hash of the IP address described above, deleted the next day. Nothing is sold, rented or shared, and no information is shared with third parties; Cloudflare runs the service as its host. The one way typed text can reach an outside site is a Try Google search that you choose to open (described above), which your browser sends to Google like any search you type yourself.

Everything described in the next section is stored by the extension on your own computer, where only you can reach it. It is disclosed here because an extension must explain how it handles data even when that data never leaves the device.

What is stored locally

Collection data below is kept in chrome.storage.local on your device.

For each card you save:

  • Card identity: name, set, the collector number its TCGplayer page prints, condition, printing, language, product and SKU ids, and whether it is a sealed product (a box, pack or tin, which has no condition).
  • Prices: current market and low price, the condition, printing and language the low price was measured at, when that low price was last seen on a listing, when the market price was last read from a page, plus a bounded history of samples, each with a timestamp, the activity that produced it and that same condition/printing/language; the condition, printing and language the market price was read at; when a page you opened had no listing at the card's condition, printing and language (which one, and when); and, for a card from an imported list, the price that list gave it and the list's date for it.
  • URLs: the TCGplayer product or listing address the card came from, and the card image address. Query strings and fragments are stripped from the saved-origin URL before storing.
  • Your own notes: free text you type on a card is kept as written.
  • Organisation: list membership, whether you marked it as a card you collected, how many copies you own (overall, and per list where they differ), a price cap if an older version stored one (no longer shown or used since 0.6.0, kept as it was), when it was saved, and where from - product page or listing page, which list you chose, and the price at that moment.

Also stored: your list names, whether each list is a deck or a collection, an optional per-list spend budget, and extension settings (junk-term filter, seller preferences, the lowest-price seller guard, price-history retention, the backup reminder, your last export choice, each selling list's asking price as a percentage of market, a vendor list's offer as a percentage of the listed price or of market, theme, dashboard defaults, and the Daily prices settings described above). On a selling list a card can also carry your own asking price for that list.

Housekeeping records. Alongside the above the extension keeps a small amount of internal bookkeeping, listed here because this page claims to enumerate everything it stores: the storage key it filed each card under and which identifier that key came from; a version marker on each card recording which release read its page; the date of your last backup export, so it can remind you when one is overdue; a one-time repair report noting when the startup consistency pass last ran and which of your own card records it corrected; per-list target values written by versions before budgets existed, kept read-only; and, for the current browser session only, the id of the dashboard tab so it can be brought to the front rather than opened twice, the id of the buy list's tab so Open next opens the next card in that same tab, the one pending imported-card choice described above - that card's storage key, the name you typed and the time - a price check you started, described above, and which cards of a buy list you have opened or skipped, kept in the dashboard tab's own session storage until that tab closes, and the dashboard's Undo steps - the cards and lists an Undo would put back - so Undo still works after a reload (emptied by erasing everything in Options; all live in session storage and are gone when the browser closes; the pending choice also clears after 30 minutes, the price check after 60). None of it describes you; it exists so the extension can find its own records and avoid repeating work.

Sealed product outlines. For a sealed product drawn by the built-in model (see Sealed product pictures), the outline it found - a few hundred bytes of shape, no pixels - keyed by the picture's address, in the extension's own IndexedDB database (tcgpartner-sealed-outlines). Not in backups; erasing everything in Options deletes it.

The extension does not ask for and does not store your name, email address, postal address, phone number, payment details, or TCGplayer account credentials.

Site access

Host access is limited to https://www.tcgplayer.com/*. It is used to:

  • Read visible page content to save card identity and visible pricing.
  • Filter visible listings by condition, and - only if you enable the junk listing filter - dim listings matching your junk terms.
  • Evaluate visible seller text to dim (never hide) listings from sellers below the thresholds you set, only if you enable seller preferences. The extension never operates a control on the page.
  • Capture price samples from TCGplayer tabs that are already open, and update local price history for saved cards on those pages.

No browsing outside tcgplayer.com is visible to the extension, and nothing about your tabs leaves your machine.

Permissions requested: storage and unlimitedStorage for your saved cards and lists, kept on this computer. The alarms permission lets the extension run the once-a-day price update. It is used for nothing else; with Daily prices off no alarm is set. (In version 0.7.0 it was an optional permission, asked for only when Daily prices were turned on.) No other host permission is requested: the price service answers the extension's requests without one.

Limited Use

TCGPartner's use of information complies with the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Data is used only to provide the extension's single purpose: keeping your own lists of TCGplayer cards - cards to buy, to build a deck with, already owned, or to sell or trade - with prices read from the TCGplayer pages you view and from TCGPartner's daily price service, and exporting those lists to files on your computer.

The extension never transfers it to third parties, other than to Cloudflare, Inc. acting only as the developer's service provider that hosts the price service. With Daily prices on (the default), TCGPartner's own price service receives the TCGplayer product numbers of your saved cards (and of a card whose TCGPartner menu you open) with your IP address, only to answer with their prices, and keeps neither, apart from a daily lookup count under a one-way hash of the IP address, deleted the next day (described above; no account, list, card names, quantities, conditions or prices are sent). Beyond that and the card pictures and searches you open, described above, nothing of yours leaves your device. It never uses or transfers it for advertising of any kind, including personalised, re-targeted or interest-based advertising, and never uses it to determine creditworthiness or for lending purposes. No human reads your data: with Daily prices off nothing reaches the developer, and with them on the price service only answers each request automatically: it keeps no product number, and keeps the IP address only as a daily one-way hash next to that day's lookup count, deleted the next day.

The buy list, and what it replaced

The extension never adds anything to your cart. The buy list is a list of ordinary links: each opens a saved card’s TCGplayer product page, filtered to that card’s condition and printing with the site’s own address options, in one tab of its own that the next card then opens in. You add it to your cart and check out yourself. Nothing is opened in a hidden tab, nothing is clicked for you, and no listing is chosen for you.

Until version 0.2.1 there was an opt-in cart helper that opened product pages in background tabs and pressed their Add to Cart control. The 3 September 2026 Terms of Service audit found that a hidden page load plus a scripted click on a commerce control is exactly what a marketplace may treat as a bot, and that the consequence would fall on your TCGplayer account. It was removed rather than kept behind a checkbox; a test in the repository fails the build if it comes back.

What runs on its own: the daily price update

The daily price update is the one thing that runs on its own; it asks TCGPartner's price service, never a TCGplayer page. Apart from it, every price the extension holds was read from a page you were looking at: nothing else runs on a schedule, nothing opens or reloads a page, and nothing is fetched from TCGplayer. With Daily prices off, a price in TCGPartner is only as fresh as your last visit to that card’s page. Every price says what it is - TCGplayer price, Lowest listing or Estimate - and how old it is once it is more than 3 days old.

Until version 0.3.0 there was an opt-in scheduled refresh that re-read prices from TCGplayer tabs you already had open, on a timer, behind a risk notice. It sent no requests, but it was automated collection of pricing information, which TCGplayer’s API terms describe as something browser add-ons must not do. It was removed outright, together with the alarms permission and its settings; an upgraded profile has them deleted on first run. The extension does not spoof identity, solve CAPTCHAs or bypass challenges.

Daily prices are different in kind, and still a timer: they never read, open or request a TCGplayer page, account or API, and no page address is sent: only the product numbers of cards you saved, and of a card whose TCGPartner menu you open (that one number does tell the service which card someone at your IP address looked at; it keeps no record of it, see above). Once a day they ask TCGPartner's price service for the prices of the product numbers you saved; the service compiles TCGplayer market prices once a day from public price data. They are on by default from version 0.8.0 (in 0.7.0 they ran only after you turned them on and read what they do), and they stop - timer and stored prices - when you turn them off.

Your control, retention and deletion

Because the data is on your machine, you hold it directly. You can view and edit every saved card and list in the dashboard, remove cards individually or in bulk, and delete whole lists. To remove everything at once, uninstall the extension - Chrome deletes its local storage, and any stored Daily prices, with it. Turning Daily prices off deletes them at once; the price service keeps only that day's lookup count under a one-way hash of the IP address, which deletes itself the next day (it cannot be looked up by name or account, so there is nothing to request). Options also has Erase everything, which deletes every saved card, list and setting on this computer after asking you to confirm.

Retention is yours to set: price-history samples are bounded and pruned according to the retention setting on the options page. The developer retains nothing that names you: with Daily prices off nothing is sent, and with them on the price service keeps only the daily lookup count under a one-way hash of the IP address, deleted the next day (described above). There is no account to close and no deletion request to submit - and equally, no way for the developer to recover data you delete.

Export writes a JSON backup to a location you choose; import reads a file you choose and merges it without deleting existing records. When you already have saved cards, import keeps your current settings; into a new install it restores the backup's settings, and the status line says which. An exported backup contains everything described above, including your notes and the stored URLs - treat the file the way you would treat the collection itself.

Version 0.7.0 and earlier could also export one collection or deck as a JSON file and let you pick the spreadsheet's columns (kept in your settings; 0.8.0 ignores and drops that choice). Such files still import: a single-list file is added as a new list or merged into one you choose. Nothing is sent anywhere.

A backup written by an older version may carry the settings of the removed scheduled refresh and its risk-notice acceptance; import drops them.

Security

Your data is protected by Chrome's own extension storage isolation and by the security of your computer and browser profile. With Daily prices off none of your data is transmitted, so there is no data of yours in transit to intercept. With Daily prices on (the default), the requests go over HTTPS, carry no cookie or credential, and only product numbers; the answers are read as data only (never run as code), and a redirected request is refused. The price service keeps no account and no record of which products anyone asked for, so there is no such database to breach; what it keeps is the day's lookup counts under daily one-way hashes of IP addresses, deleted the next day.

Two honest limits: anyone with access to your unlocked computer and browser profile can read the extension's data, and an exported backup file is a plain JSON file with no encryption. Store backups accordingly.

Do Not Track

TCGPartner does not track you across websites or over time, and does not build any profile of you. Because it performs no tracking at all, there is nothing for a browser “Do Not Track” signal to switch off, and the extension does not alter its behaviour in response to one.

No other party conducts tracking through this extension. This disclosure is provided under California Business and Professions Code section 22575(b)(5).

International users

The extension is available wherever the Chrome Web Store is. Wherever you are, the position is the same: with Daily prices off, the developer receives no personal data from you, and nothing personal leaves your device. With Daily prices on (the default), TCGPartner's price service (hosted by Cloudflare, Inc.) receives with each request your IP address, which the GDPR and the UK GDPR treat as personal data, and the TCGplayer product numbers of your saved cards (or of the one card whose TCGPartner menu you opened). They are used only to answer that request; apart from the daily lookup count kept under a one-way hash of the IP address (pseudonymous data, not anonymous) and deleted the next day, nothing of it is kept. Turning Daily prices off stops these requests.

For that processing, the developer of TCGPartner, Alex Heldt, is the controller, reachable through the support address under Contact below. The purpose is showing current prices for the cards you saved, which is the main thing the extension is for, and limiting abuse; the basis is the developer's legitimate interest in providing that function (the menu lookup is part of it: it shows a card's printings so you can save the one you mean).

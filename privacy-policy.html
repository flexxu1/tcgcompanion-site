TCGCompanion Privacy Policy

TCGCompanion is a local-only Chrome extension. It has no account, no server, and no sync. Everything it records stays on your machine until you delete it or export it yourself.

Effective date: 12 September 2026 · Applies to TCGCompanion version 0.2.1 and later (first published 19 August 2026 for 0.1.0; the cart helper described in earlier versions was removed in 0.2.1). Last verified against the extension source on 12 September 2026.

Network activity: none

The extension makes no network request of its own, with one exception stated plainly under Card pictures below: showing a saved card's picture loads that image from tcgplayer.com. There is no external server, cloud sync, analytics, advertising, telemetry, or crash reporting, and no fetch, XMLHttpRequest, WebSocket or sendBeacon call anywhere in the shipped code. If that ever changes, this policy will be updated before the change ships - see Changes to this policy below.

Deck import works from what you type. When you run Import a deck, the card names, copies, printing numbers and set codes you pasted are saved exactly as typed, into a deck on your machine; nothing is looked up anywhere. Cards added this way store three extra fields: where the record came from (importedFrom), the printing number you typed (importNumber) and the set code you typed (importSetHint), each only when present. These are identity only - an imported card carries no price and no picture until you open its TCGplayer page yourself, exactly like any other unpriced card. Before version 0.4.0 (2026-09-12) deck import sent the typed names to a third-party card database to offer a printing picker; that call, its host permission and its stored fields are gone, and records written by earlier versions are rewritten to the typed shape on first run.

Prices are read from TCGplayer pages you already have open in your own browser session. The extension never fetches TCGplayer pages by itself.

Card pictures. When the dashboard shows a card's picture, your browser loads that image from the address stored with the card - TCGplayer's own image servers - the same request the site itself makes when you browse. Nothing but the image address is sent. That is the only host ever loaded from; a backup naming any other host renders “No image” instead.

What the developer collects: nothing

The developer does not collect, receive, transmit, sell, rent, or share any user data. There is no category of personal information gathered by the developer, and there are no third parties with whom information is shared, because no information ever leaves your device.

Everything described in the next section is stored by the extension on your own computer, where only you can reach it. It is disclosed here because an extension must explain how it handles data even when that data never leaves the device.

What is stored locally

Everything below is kept in chrome.storage.local on your device.

For each card you save:

  • Card identity: name, set, condition, printing, language, product and SKU ids.
  • Prices: current market and low price, plus a bounded history of samples, each with a timestamp and the activity that produced it.
  • URLs: the TCGplayer product or listing address the card came from, and the card image address. Query strings and fragments are stripped from the saved-origin URL before storing.
  • Your own notes: free text you type on a card is kept as written.
  • Organisation: list membership, whether you marked it as a card you already have, how many copies you own, an optional cart price cap, when it was saved, and where from - product page or listing page, which list you chose, and the price at that moment.

Also stored: your list names, whether each list is a deck or a collection, an optional per-list spend budget, and extension settings (junk-term filter, seller preferences, the lowest-price seller guard, price-history retention, whether the extension draws its controls on TCGplayer pages, the backup reminder, theme, and dashboard defaults).

Housekeeping records. Alongside the above the extension keeps a small amount of internal bookkeeping, listed here because this page claims to enumerate everything it stores: the storage key it filed each card under and which identifier that key came from; a version marker on each card recording which release read its page; the date of your last backup export, so it can remind you when one is overdue; a one-time repair report noting when the startup consistency pass last ran and which of your own card records it corrected; per-list target values written by versions before budgets existed, kept read-only; and, for the current browser session only, the id of the dashboard tab so it can be brought to the front rather than opened twice (that one lives in session storage and is gone when the browser closes). None of it describes you; it exists so the extension can find its own records and avoid repeating work.

The extension does not ask for and does not store your name, email address, postal address, phone number, payment details, or TCGplayer account credentials.

Site access

Host access is limited to https://*.tcgplayer.com/*. It is used to:

  • Read visible page content to save card identity and visible pricing.
  • Filter visible listings by condition, and - only if you enable the junk listing filter - dim listings matching your junk terms.
  • Evaluate visible seller text to dim (never hide) listings from sellers below the thresholds you set, only if you enable seller preferences. The extension never operates a control on the page.
  • Capture price samples from TCGplayer tabs that are already open, and update local price history for saved cards on those pages.

No browsing outside tcgplayer.com is visible to the extension, and nothing about your tabs leaves your machine.

Permissions requested: storage and unlimitedStorage for local shortlist data. Nothing else - in particular there is no alarms permission, so the extension has no way to run on a timer.

Limited Use

TCGCompanion's use of information complies with the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. Data is used only to provide the extension's single purpose - a local TCGplayer card watchlist with listing filters, price snapshots and a buy list of links to the cards you still need.

It is never transferred to third parties, never used or transferred for advertising of any kind, including personalised, re-targeted or interest-based advertising, and never used to determine creditworthiness or for lending purposes. No human reads your data, because no data reaches the developer.

The buy list, and what it replaced

The extension never adds anything to your cart. The buy list is a list of ordinary links: each opens a saved card’s TCGplayer product page in a new tab, exactly as the site serves it. You add it to your cart and check out yourself. Nothing is opened in a hidden tab, nothing is clicked for you, and no listing is chosen for you.

Until version 0.2.1 there was an opt-in cart helper that opened product pages in background tabs and pressed their Add to Cart control. The 3 September 2026 Terms of Service audit found that a hidden page load plus a scripted click on a commerce control is exactly what a marketplace may treat as a bot, and that the consequence would fall on your TCGplayer account. It was removed rather than kept behind a checkbox; a test in the repository fails the build if it comes back.

Nothing runs without you

Every price the extension holds was read from a page you were looking at. Nothing runs on a schedule, nothing opens or reloads a page, and nothing is fetched from TCGplayer. A price on the dashboard is therefore only as fresh as your last visit to that card’s page, and a list budget is an estimate built from those last-seen prices.

Until version 0.3.0 there was an opt-in scheduled refresh that re-read prices from TCGplayer tabs you already had open, on a timer, behind a risk notice. It sent no requests, but it was automated collection of pricing information, which TCGplayer’s API terms describe as something browser add-ons must not do. It was removed outright, together with the alarms permission and its settings; an upgraded profile has them deleted on first run. The extension does not spoof identity, solve CAPTCHAs or bypass challenges.

Your control, retention and deletion

Because the data is on your machine, you hold it directly. You can view and edit every saved card and list in the dashboard, remove cards individually or in bulk, and delete whole lists. To remove everything at once, uninstall the extension - Chrome deletes its local storage with it. There is no in-product “erase all data” button.

Retention is yours to set: price-history samples are bounded and pruned according to the retention setting on the options page. Nothing is retained by the developer, because nothing is sent to the developer. There is no account to close and no deletion request to submit - and equally, no way for the developer to recover data you delete.

Export writes a JSON backup to a location you choose; import reads a file you choose and merges it without deleting existing records. An exported backup contains everything described above, including your notes and the stored URLs - treat the file the way you would treat the collection itself.

A backup written by an older version may carry the settings of the removed scheduled refresh and its risk-notice acceptance; import drops them.

Security

Your data is protected by Chrome's own extension storage isolation and by the security of your computer and browser profile. Because nothing is transmitted, there is no server to breach, no database of users, and no data in transit to intercept.

Two honest limits: anyone with access to your unlocked computer and browser profile can read the extension's data, and an exported backup file is a plain JSON file with no encryption. Store backups accordingly.

Do Not Track

TCGCompanion does not track you across websites or over time, and does not build any profile of you. Because it performs no tracking at all, there is nothing for a browser “Do Not Track” signal to switch off, and the extension does not alter its behaviour in response to one.

No other party conducts tracking through this extension. This disclosure is provided under California Business and Professions Code section 22575(b)(5).

International users

The extension is available wherever the Chrome Web Store is. Wherever you are, the position is the same: the developer receives no personal data from you and therefore does not process any. For readers in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, that means the developer is not a controller or processor of your personal data under the GDPR or the UK GDPR in respect of this extension, and there is no transfer of personal data off your device. The rights those laws give you - to access, correct, export and erase your data - are exercised directly, in the dashboard and through the export and uninstall steps above, without asking anyone.

For readers in California and other US states with consumer privacy laws: the developer does not sell or share personal information, does not use it for targeted advertising or profiling, and has no personal information about you to disclose, correct or delete on request. No opt-out is needed because there is nothing to opt out of.

Children

TCGCompanion is a general-audience tool and is not directed to children under 13. The developer does not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13 - and in fact collects no personal information from anyone, as described above.

Changes to this policy

Material changes to what the extension does with your data will also be disclosed in the extension's own interface and in the Chrome Web Store listing, so that a change in data handling is never applied silently to an existing install.
